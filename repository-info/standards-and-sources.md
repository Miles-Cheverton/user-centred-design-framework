# Standards and sources

**Source review date:** 2026-11-16

This document records the guidance used to shape the starter. It is not a claim of GDS endorsement, a complete assessment checklist or evidence that a particular service meets a standard.

The linked sources describe practices and expectations. The filenames, workbook columns, ID conventions and folder layout are this template’s implementation choices. Government requirements must be interpreted in the context in which they apply; teams using the starter elsewhere should identify the standards and obligations relevant to their service.

| Concern | Primary guidance | How it informs the starter |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-service quality | [GDS Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard) | User needs, joined-up channels, multidisciplinary work, iteration, privacy, performance and reliable operation are represented across the records. |
| Problem framing, scope and constraints | [How discovery works](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works) | State the underlying problem, challenge a preselected solution, understand boundaries and distinguish constraints that can be changed. |
| User groups and needs | [Learning about users and their needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs) | Needs describe what people need to achieve and why, use recognisable language and link to evidence. Include people who provide or support the service. |
| Delivery stories | [Writing user stories](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/writing-user-stories) | Keep links from delivery work to needs. Detailed story acceptance criteria remain with the delivery records. |
| Research planning | [Plan user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service) | Plan useful rounds around questions, methods, participants and decisions. Each round has a practical protocol. |
| Evidence and findings | [Analyse a research session](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/analyse-a-research-session) | Separate observations from interpreted findings and chosen actions. |
| Consent | [Getting informed consent](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/getting-users-consent-for-research) | Keep matchable evidence of the consent applicable to research activity and data use. |
| Research data | [Managing research data and participant privacy](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/managing-user-research-data-participant-privacy) | Minimise data, restrict access, retain it only as agreed and link coded participants, sessions and material carefully. |
| Journeys | [Creating an experience map](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/creating-an-experience-map) | Represent users’ experience across stages and channels; revise maps as understanding develops. |
| Diagram access | [W3C complex images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/) | Supply a checked text equivalent conveying a journey graphic’s essential information and relationships. |
| Prototypes | [Making prototypes](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/making-prototypes) | Explore and test approaches at an appropriate fidelity; retain the exact version referenced by research. |
| Outcomes and measures | [Setting performance metrics](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/how-to-set-performance-metrics-for-your-service) | Define intended benefits, meaningful measures, sources, baselines and targets. |
| Team roles | [What each role does](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/the-team/what-each-role-does-in-service-team) | Identify relevant multidisciplinary responsibilities and the people or teams carrying them. |
| Proportionate governance | [Agile service governance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/governance-principles-for-agile-service-delivery) | Documentation should support decisions, ownership and useful action. |
| Risks | [HM Treasury Orange Book](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/orange-book/the-orange-book-management-of-risk-principles-and-concepts) | Explain cause, event and consequence; assess operating controls and assign treatment and review. |
| Decision reasoning | [Michael Nygard’s decision records](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions) | Adapt the short context, decision, status and consequences pattern beyond architecture. |
| Non-functional requirements | [Defra digital service manual](https://digital.defra.gov.uk/business-analysis/non-functional-requirements) | Use the established government delivery term for service-wide qualities such as performance and reliability. This is departmental guidance, not a universal GDS filename mandate. |
| Accessibility verification | [GDS accessibility testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/testing-for-accessibility) | Identify applicable standards and combine appropriate checks with research involving people with access needs. |
| Business rules terminology | [DfT street manager business rules](https://department-for-transport-streetmanager.github.io/street-manager-docs/articles/business-rules-version-2-00-service.html) | An example of the term in a public service. It does not mandate this starter’s register. |
| AI retrieval context | [Anthropic: contextual retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/research/contextual-retrieval) | Supports preserving context when extracting records and evaluating the chosen retrieval approach. The reference fields and example questions are framework choices, not a claim that this structure reproduces the research results. |

The downloadable starter also links to the professional sources the AI skills use for advice, including standards from Australia, Canada and Singapore, W3C accessibility guidance and the Scrum and Kanban guides. ## Keep the relationship current

When guidance changes, check which recommendations, fields, examples and prompts are affected. Record the meaningful change in the changelog and update the review date after checking the source. Do not describe a page as newly reviewed merely because its link still opens.

The underlying user needs, evidence and human decisions remain the project’s own work. A source citation supports the practice; it does not supply missing project evidence.
