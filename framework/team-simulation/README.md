# Team simulation and framework assessment

**Original assessed draft:** 0.3.0 · **Guidance revision:** 0.3.1 · **Date:** 16 November 2026

The framework has a strong foundation for shared project memory: clear concept names, explicit sources, retained disagreement and human decisions. Its greatest remaining risk is that tidy records can look more certain or complete than the work they represent. The 0.3.1 guidance changes address the working instructions without adding registers or ceremonies. Their effectiveness still needs real-team validation.

This is a detailed fictional walkthrough informed by published research, practitioner accounts and GDS guidance. It is not a trial with a real team, an independent service assessment or evidence of faster delivery. The dialogue, people, service results and record changes in the stories are invented. The blank template has not been populated with these examples.

**Human analysis is the starting point.** Following Miles's clarification, the revised scenes explicitly require the researcher and team to discuss observations, examine contradictions and draw conclusions before AI tidies, checks or records those conclusions. An AI-generated interpretation with a human approval step does not meet this working rule. The original simulation and checks remain historical evidence about v0.3.0; revising the examples is not a new trial.

The fictional council service lends community equipment. Its experience includes telephone and online enquiries, depot preparation, collections, returns and support. Eleven colleagues contribute product, delivery, research, interaction design, service design, content, development, analysis, sponsorship and operations expertise.

## What the external evidence changes

The sources provide reasons to test these situations, rather than proof that this template solves them.

- **Speaking up is part of the work.** Edmondson's field study connects psychological safety with learning behaviour. The role-play therefore includes a newer developer noticing a consequential problem and a senior colleague having to respond constructively. A new field cannot create that response. [Original study](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Group_Performance/Edmondson%20Psychological%20safety.pdf).
- **Useful research needs shared interpretation.** GDS and Parliamentary Digital Service accounts describe observation, joint analysis and discussion of actions. This supports keeping findings inside project memory while using existing team conversations to work out their implications. [GDS account](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2013/08/30/how-we-do-user-research-in-agile-teams/), [Parliamentary account](https://pds.blog.parliament.uk/2016/12/05/what-we-learned-from-the-gds-user-research-team/).
- **Process can encourage compliance instead of thought.** A GDS designer's account warns about gatekeeping. The simulation tests whether a record helps a decision or merely encourages someone to complete a box. [Practitioner account](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2021/03/02/three-things-product-teams-need-from-designers/).
- **AI fluency can invite excessive trust.** A survey of knowledge workers associates confidence in AI with lower self-reported critical-thinking effort. This motivates testing polished summaries that conceal uncertainty; it does not establish that AI inevitably reduces judgement. [Research paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/).

## Where the framework helps

**People can distinguish facts that are often collapsed together.** A sponsor's preferred solution, an observed need, a promising proposal, an agreed decision and a verified result have different homes. In discovery this stops the supplied brief from becoming proof that an app is needed. In alpha it stops a selected prototype from becoming implied release approval.

**The project can retain its learning without cataloguing every thought.** Contrary and inconclusive findings remain alongside positive findings. Significant discarded options retain their rationale. Disposable sketches can stay disposable, while the insight they produced is captured when substantive. This balance is essential to creative work.

**AI can support retrieval and recording.** The portable skills give it a route from conclusions already reached by people to the correct record, with stable IDs and evidence. It can assemble an answer or handover from existing owners, avoiding another maintained summary. A raw research source is insufficient to generate a finding: the human discussion and conclusions come first. The assistant preserves those conclusions instead of producing an AI synthesis for the team to approve.

**Need coverage supports honest trade-offs.** The separate assessment and delivery-decision fields allow a team to say “partly met and we will address the gap” or “not met and we will not address it in this scope”. Neither choice converts an unmet need into success. The beta example makes this distinction concrete across online and telephone routes.

## Where it may become difficult

**There is still a substantial catalogue.** The template contains 23 working registers, an allowed-values workbook, narrative records, guides and ten skills. A newcomer presented with all of them can reasonably feel overwhelmed. The task-based start and staged capture are therefore core functionality. The framework should be used through the work at hand, not as an exercise to fill every file.

**Recording is not communication.** A correct shared file does not establish that operations, content or an absent colleague understands a changed promise. The team needs to discuss consequential changes through its existing work. SharePoint resolves the editing assumption; it does not substitute for that conversation.

**Completeness has limits.** An integrity check can find missing IDs, unresolved links and known synthesis gaps. It cannot discover an insight nobody shared or prove that every relevant user was heard. “No structural faults found” must never mean “we understand the service completely”.

**Respect matters as much as correct classification.** A designer's intuition may be worth exploring before there is evidence. Calling it an assumption should help the team test it, not dismiss it. AI should preserve attribution, explain uncertainty plainly and invite correction. It should not grade individuals, diagnose personalities or infer consent from silence.

**The tool needs the right access.** Model-neutral instructions are portable, but not every AI interface can read full Excel tables, inspect an interaction or access restricted research. The assistant must expose that limitation. A source pointer is not proof it inspected the source. This is a capability boundary, not a reason to add a second editable file format.

## Guidance changes in 0.3.1

Miles asked for these refinements to be implemented. They are incorporated into the existing guidance and skills; the core schema and register count stay unchanged. The human-analysis rule is an explicit project requirement. The examples and task routes support that rule without introducing another meeting, approval log or status report. These are implementation changes, not evidence that adoption or service outcomes have improved.

Staged capture, single owners, retained contrary findings, portable skills and separate need-coverage dimensions were already in v0.3.0. The revisions clarify how people use them and when AI may assist, rather than requiring additional project records.

## What the AI co-pilot should do

After user testing, the researcher and team meet to compare observations, consider different explanations, discuss contradictions and reach their own conclusions. Those conclusions can be captured in their ordinary discussion notes; no separate proof-of-analysis register is needed.

The assistant can then tidy those supplied conclusions without changing their meaning, locate the cited source passages, check quotations and version references, and flag a mismatch or omitted contradictory passage for human attention. It must not resolve the interpretation itself. When authorised, it saves the records and verifies the affected links. Draft means the documentation is unfinished; it does not permit AI-authored research analysis.

If given only a raw transcript, the assistant can retain the source and literal observations when requested, and identify that human analysis is still needed. It should not produce themes, findings or user needs for people to rubber-stamp. Existing human discussion, notes or project records can establish the analysis; asking for another confirmation is unnecessary when the meaning and authority are already clear.

Before a review, it can assemble existing decisions, recorded dependencies and unresolved questions. It can calculate defined totals and detect structural gaps. It should preserve people's tentative ideas and attribution, express source discrepancies neutrally and leave the interpretation of those discrepancies to them.

It should not turn every discussion into forms, run a full audit after every small edit, manufacture consensus, judge a need met because a feature shipped, or make a release decision. The team can delegate routine recording and mechanical repair; it performs the critical thinking, analysis and synthesis and retains priorities, creative direction and accountability.

Several fictional conflicts end with constructive responses. That demonstrates the intended working pattern, not a prediction. If powerful colleagues suppress contrary evidence or refuse to involve operations, a well-organised folder cannot resolve that behaviour. The framework should preserve the relevant uncertainty without becoming a staff-surveillance system.

## What was actually checked

The original phase simulations inspected the actual v0.3.0 structures, guides and relevant skills; beta also inspected the coverage workbook fields and formulas. These inspections support whether the described records exist, not whether a real team would use them successfully. The 0.3.1 edits clarify intended behaviour and do not rerun or upgrade these checks.

Separate [AI-usability results](../ai-usability/results.md) record actual checks:

- Schema, reference and local-link checks of the saved files.
- Need-coverage calculations exercised with temporary populated inputs and recalculated in LibreOffice. Native Microsoft Excel interaction was not tested.
- An isolated integrity-skill run that repaired established structural faults and left missing evidence and judgement gaps unresolved. Its changes were checked independently.
- A historical six-question v0.2.0 read test; it was not relabelled as a v0.3.0 or cross-model benchmark.

No real multidisciplinary team has trialled this framework. No measured claim of improved service quality, productivity or adoption follows from the role-play.

## Practical validation next

Use the existing template with a real small team and a team with part-time or distributed contributors. Observe ordinary work rather than introducing a separate reporting programme: capture one messy meeting, synthesise one research round, retain a changed design and prepare a real decision or handover.

Watch whether colleagues can find the right home without reading all the guides; do the analysis themselves before using AI to document it; correct an inaccurate representation comfortably; preserve contrary learning; trace a claim to its actual version; and identify an unmet need across channels. Note the time and interruptions caused by recording, especially repeated clarification and duplicated entry. Compare two AI environments with different file-access capabilities before making broader portability claims.

Prioritise failures that could distort a service decision or exclude users, followed by recurring friction. Simplify instructions or examples before adding fields. Treat these as proposed pilot activities, not completed tests or newly scheduled commitments.
