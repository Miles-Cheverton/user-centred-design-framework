# Evidence informing the team simulation

Reviewed 16 November 2026. These sources inform plausible situations; none evaluates this template. The role-play, dialogue, service records and proposed changes are constructed examples. Empirical research, practitioner experience and official guidance support different kinds of claim.

| Source | Evidence and useful observation | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **S1. Amy Edmondson, [Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Group_Performance/Edmondson%20Psychological%20safety.pdf), 1999** | Original field study of 51 teams. Psychological safety was associated with learning behaviours such as discussing errors and seeking help. Informs scenes about speaking up and revising an earlier view. | One manufacturing organisation; association does not prove a documentation or AI intervention will improve safety. |
| **S2. Longqi Yang and colleagues, [The effects of remote work on collaboration among information workers](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-effects-of-remote-work-on-collaboration-among-information-workers/), 2021 online / 2022 journal** | Study of communication records for 61,182 Microsoft employees, using quasi-experimental analysis. The authors estimated more siloed networks and greater asynchronous communication after the remote-work shift. Informs missed handoffs. | One company during the pandemic. Does not establish that remote teams generally perform worse or that more meetings are the answer. |
| **S3. Hao-Ping Lee and colleagues, [The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/), CHI 2025** | Survey of 319 knowledge workers. Confidence in AI was associated with lower self-reported critical-thinking effort. Informs scenes about accepting polished summaries and apparent certainty. | Self-report and association; not proof of cognitive decline or causal performance effects. |
| **S4. Chris Hill-Scott, [Three things product teams need from designers](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2021/03/02/three-things-product-teams-need-from-designers/), 2 March 2021** | First-hand GDS practitioner account. Warns that design gatekeeping can encourage minimal compliance; discusses making ideas visible, contributing to production and service coherence. | Professional reflection, not a controlled study or mandatory standard. |
| **S5. Leisa Reichelt, [How we do user research in agile teams](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2013/08/30/how-we-do-user-research-in-agile-teams/), 30 August 2013** | First-hand account of embedded research and shared analysis. Retains findings in a research library, with resulting work in the backlog; reduces lengthy reports to preserve collaboration. | Historical operating experience; its particular tools and cadence are not universal requirements. |
| **S6. Steve Bromley, [What we learned from the GDS User Research team](https://pds.blog.parliament.uk/2016/12/05/what-we-learned-from-the-gds-user-research-team/), 5 December 2016** | Parliamentary Digital Service observation report. Stakeholders observed sessions, helped interpret observations with transcripts available, then discussed actions. Informs the distinction between evidence, interpretation and decisions. | A favourable account of one visit, not comparative evidence of effectiveness. |
| **S7. Ann Oduwaiye, Jeremy Yun and Rose Westcott, [A day in the life of an interaction designer at GDS](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2022/03/25/a-day-in-the-life-of-an-interaction-designer-at-gds/), 25 March 2022** | Three practitioner accounts covering research, prototyping, content pairing, developer collaboration and learning as a new colleague. Informs onboarding and cross-discipline handoffs. | Institutional recruitment narratives; not representative evidence of every team's culture. |
| **S8. Cabinet Office, [Test, Learn and Grow Playbook: Ways of working](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/test-learn-and-grow-playbook/ways-of-working), 19 October 2026** | Official guidance recognises different jargon, methods and tools, and recommends clear decision boundaries and proportionate governance. Informs disagreement over roles and process. | Practice guidance, not empirical proof that this framework will improve delivery; complements rather than replaces the Service Standard. |

## GDS context for the simulated phases

- [Discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works): investigate the problem, users, policy context and constraints before committing to a service solution.
- [Alpha](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-alpha-phase-works): explore and test approaches and use what is learned to decide whether and how to proceed.
- [Beta](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-beta-phase-works): develop and improve the service with users in its operating context.
- [Multidisciplinary service teams](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/the-team/set-up-a-service-team): assemble the skills appropriate to the phase and service; the fictional cast is illustrative, not a universal staffing prescription.
- [Agile governance principles](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/governance-principles-for-agile-service-delivery): documentation and controls should support decisions and add value.
- [Sharing research findings](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/sharing-user-research-findings): make learning useful to the team so it can improve the service.

## Interpretation discipline

The simulation uses these sources to pose challenges. It does not claim that any fictional exchange happened, that a particular profession has a fixed personality, or that the proposed changes will work for every team. Keep disagreement about work visible without creating AI judgements about individual personalities. Validate the framework with real teams before making effectiveness or speed claims.
