# Record a project item

Follow the [shared skill rules](../README.md). Use a specialised skill for needs, use cases, findings, assumptions, decisions, design options or need coverage.

## Supported records

This procedure covers user groups, outcomes, stakeholders, team members, scope items, constraints, dependencies, risks, research questions/rounds/sessions, coded participants, business rules, non-functional requirements, glossary terms and supplied-document records. It does not invent a new record type or register.

For discussion guides, questionnaires, response exports, analysis working or Whiteboards, use [Research materials](#research-materials) below and the [research-protocol guide](../../how-to/research-protocol.md). These are files supporting a round or design, not extra central registers.

## Procedure

1. Identify the intended concept and read its guide under how-to/files/. If classification changes the meaning—such as constraint versus assumption—resolve that distinction from the source or retain an explicit question.
2. Read the current owner and exact schema. Check for an existing matching record and stable ID. Use the guide’s small first capture, then complete the fields needed before the record is used.
3. Extract literal facts and the team's stated analysis or decisions, then resolve typed references through the canonical relationship table. Do not derive user groups, outcomes, risks or conclusions by synthesising raw research. Human judgement supplies their meaning; AI may check, tidy and record it. Keep narrative authority citations distinct from ID-only fields.
4. Apply the concept’s particular conditions: scope belongs in one current scope list; a negotiable constraint needs its change condition; participant research needs applicable consent; a rule needs its authority; requirement agreement and verification are separate. Do not infer any of these from a document title or senior speaker alone.
5. Save the bounded record when authorised and verify its references. Preserve unrelated rows, file controls and meaningful history.

## Research materials

1. Establish the round or design this material supports and read its protocol or description. Use that subject's folder and existing links. In a research round, the starter names are discussion-guide.md, survey-questionnaire.md, survey-responses.xlsx, survey-analysis.xlsx, analysis-notes.md and whiteboard.md. Use the actual export format instead of forcing the response workbook. Do not populate unused templates merely to complete the folder.
2. For a guide or questionnaire, tidy the researcher’s supplied questions, tasks, answer options and branching without changing their intended meaning. Preserve the instrument version actually used. Refer to survey questions by their scoped question ID plus questionnaire version; do not turn question wording into a finding or invent missing research-design choices.
3. For survey responses, preserve the exact original export and capture its export date/version and authoritative location using the existing protocol/source route. Do not overwrite it with cleaned data, re-enter it into the starter, or create a second editable copy merely to match the filename. Register a DOC source when observations or findings need to cite the dataset; no SES per respondent or new global response-ID scheme is needed.
4. For survey-analysis working, use the team's stated calculation method and the actual workbook headers. Record the exact input dataset and instrument version, question or option, included population/filter, base or denominator, missing-response handling and relevant limits. Preserve calculations separately from raw data. Check arithmetic against the defined method; never choose exclusions, weights, a denominator or interpretation silently. A multi-select total is not automatically a count of people, and a blank is not zero. Leave unspecified choices for the researcher while completing independent authorised filing.
5. For a Whiteboard, record its canonical working link, purpose and subject. When its substantive state supports analysis or a decision, reference the relevant retained snapshot and exact capture/version in the approved location. Read only what tools can actually access; disclose missing canvas areas or inaccessible content. Retain the team's supplied analysis in analysis-notes.md or its existing owner, without inferring themes or conclusions from the board. Design boards stay with the relevant journey or prototype.
6. Link meaningful non-session sources through the existing document register when needed for citation. For EV records, cite the source's exact version and a useful locator: dataset sheet/question/rows or cells, document section, or identifiable board area/item in a retained snapshot. A mutable dashboard, folder or live board link alone cannot establish the historical content used. Preserve access restrictions and material contrary observations.
7. Save the authorised material and check the round/source links. Human conclusions remain in research-findings.md, and consequential choices in decisions.md. Use the specialised skills to document conclusions already reached; raw results or a completed calculation do not authorise AI analysis.

## Result

Return the owner file, record ID and what changed, plus unresolved facts affecting use. Do not send messages, create commitments, collect unnecessary personal information or start research as a side effect of recording an item.
