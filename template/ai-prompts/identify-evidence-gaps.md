# Identify evidence gaps

## Supply

- **Target:** [Proposal, decision, user need, journey or service area]
- **Decision at stake:** [What the team needs to decide]
- **Scope:** [Versions, user groups, time period and any boundary]

## Prompt

Read README.md, ai-guidance.md and the canonical relationship reference in how-to/record-conventions.md, then the relevant file guides. Examine the evidence supporting the target within the supplied scope, without reading every project file indiscriminately.

This is a source and record consistency check. People perform the research analysis and decide what the evidence means. Compare the team's stated claims with exact sources and recorded scope; flag discrepancies or missing support without supplying alternative findings, explanations or assessments. Do not conduct thematic analysis of raw transcripts.

Trace relevant links from claims to findings and source observations. Check whether the evidence covers the stated user groups, circumstances and version. Consider untested assumptions, contradictory findings, outdated material and unsupported generalisation.

Use exact typed IDs and relationship fields. Preserve ID plus Version for journeys and prototypes and follow explicit Superseded by references. A similar title or statement does not resolve an ID. Check each material source using Source ID, Source version or path and Locator in evidence.xlsx.

Workbook coverage must include the actual sheet/table, headers and relevant full rows. Preserve ID, status, version and source paths in extracts. A partial preview cannot prove an absent record. Use the live project’s records as evidence.

Distinguish:

- a specific mismatch or missing support located when checking the team's claim;
- a source that is recorded but inaccessible to you;
- a missing record;
- a question outside the agreed scope.

These are different limitations. Do not describe an inaccessible source as absent evidence.

Find reverse relationships by querying their owning fields: findings hold Research question IDs, Round or session IDs and Design versions; prototypes hold Proposal IDs. For a round, include findings linked directly to its RND ID or through its SES records. Do not maintain duplicate backlinks. Include substantive contradictory and inconclusive findings, not only support for a preferred answer.

If the question concerns whether needs are met, distinguish user-needs.xlsx evidential Status from user-need-coverage.xlsx Assessment and Delivery decision. Validate assessed claims against EV support, date and exact version/scope. Reconcile each active UN to one current coverage row before claiming project-wide coverage; Summary counts are recorded-row counts across independent dimensions, not completeness or a percentage.

Return a short prioritised list. For each gap give:

1. The claim or decision affected, with its record reference.
2. What was checked and what remains uncertain.
3. Why the uncertainty matters to the stated decision.
4. A specific source check or question for human judgement that could clarify it.

Identify references that resolved as well as gaps; a successful source check does not establish research adequacy. Do not produce an exhaustive generic research checklist, assign false numerical confidence or create new commitments. A source-check suggestion can help the team; the team determines any further research and its method.

## Human check

The team decides which uncertainties matter enough to investigate and records any chosen research question, assumption or decision in its owning file.
