# Problem statement

**Working file:** [problem-statement.md](../../problem-statement.md)

The working file contains the problem statement only. Use this guide to develop it; keep evidence, scope, outcomes and solutions in their own records.

## First capture

Write one sentence describing the difficulty people experience. Refine it with the evidence before treating it as established; keep background, scope and solutions in their own records.

## Complete the statement

1. Identify the people experiencing the problem and the situation in which it arises.
2. Explain what is difficult, failing or missing and why it matters.
3. Challenge a commission that already names a solution. Ask what underlying problem that solution is supposed to address.
4. Write one concise statement in plain language. Remove proposed features, delivery plans and background narrative.
5. Check it with the team against the evidence. Link that evidence from the evidence register or relevant finding.

GDS supplies problem-framing guidance, not a universal mandatory sentence formula.

## Fictional example

> Applicants cannot tell when their application is likely to be decided, making it difficult to arrange the next steps that depend on that decision.

This is an example of wording, not a verified problem for the project.

## Keep it current

Refine the wording as understanding develops. Preserve the original mandate in the document register. If the framing changes materially, use the decision’s Context for the mandate DOC ID and exact passage plus the previous wording or exact retained version. Use Choice for the new framing, Rationale for the findings that prompted it, and Consequences for effects on scope, outcomes or research priorities. Keep the concise current statement in the working file.

## Completion check

- It describes a problem experienced by identifiable people.
- It explains the consequence.
- It does not prescribe an app, channel or other solution.
- The team can point to supporting evidence elsewhere.
