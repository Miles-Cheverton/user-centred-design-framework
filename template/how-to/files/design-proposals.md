# Design proposals

**Working file:** [design-proposals.md](../../design-proposals.md)

A proposal describes an option for changing the service. Keep alternative ideas visible while they are explored; a proposal is not an agreed decision or an implemented service.


## First capture

Save a stable PROP ID, the proposed change and Draft status. Register substantive options retained for research, a significant decision, shared review or handover. Disposable sketches can stay in normal design tools; their substantive learning must still be recorded.

## Complete a proposal

1. Describe the option in terms of the service change it would produce.
2. Connect it to relevant needs and intended outcomes.
3. Identify what needs to be learned before committing to it.
4. Cite retained non-prototype design material here. Register prototypes with this proposal in their Proposal IDs field; retrieve that list from the register.
5. Capture all substantive evaluation findings in research-findings.md, then explain what they mean for this option and record its actual disposition.

## Fictional example

**PROP-001 — Explain the next decision window**

Explore providing a realistic decision window after an application is submitted. It addresses UN-001 and OUT-001. Test whether applicants understand the wording and whether operations can supply dependable timing. A retained prototype is registered with Proposal IDs = PROP-001; it is found there rather than copied into a second list. The option is Untested.

## Fields and sections

Use the small first capture to save a draft. Complete the relevant fields before relying on it; an unfinished record does not establish evidence, agreement or verification.

| Field or section | Needed when | What belongs here |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal ID and name | At capture | Stable PROP ID and recognisable name. |
| Status | At capture; update when disposition changes | Draft, Exploring, Selected, Discarded or Superseded. |
| Superseded by | When replacements exist | Exact replacement PROP IDs separated by semicolons; blank on a Superseded proposal is an unresolved gap. |
| User need IDs | When related needs are established | Exact UN IDs the option addresses. |
| Outcome IDs | When related outcomes are established | Exact OUT IDs for intended benefits. |
| Journey versions | When the proposal concerns retained journeys | Exact JNY IDs and versions. |
| Proposed change | At capture | The change to people, channels or operations. |
| Why explore it | Before substantive review or testing | The motivation and recorded FND, EV, UN or ASM support; assumptions remain unproven. |
| Design and prototype | When design material is retained | Cite exact non-prototype artefact locations. Find prototypes by filtering prototypes.xlsx Proposal IDs for this PROP ID; do not author a prototype list here. |
| What to test | Before testing | The uncertainty; cite recorded RQ or ASM IDs where relevant. |
| What we learned | After evaluation | Explain implications for this option using FND support; otherwise state Untested. All substantive findings remain in research-findings.md. |
| Disposition | When selected, discarded or replaced | The actual choice and rationale, with its recorded DEC attribution. Flag missing decisions; Selected does not mean implemented. |

## Keep it current

Retain worthwhile discarded alternatives and their rationale. Preserve versions used in research. Selected identifies a design direction, not proof of implementation. Use Superseded by for every exact replacement PROP ID; no self-links or cycles.

## Completion check

The proposal has a purpose, an uncertainty to investigate and an honest status. It is possible to trace any selection to a decision.

## Practice basis

[GDS: making prototypes](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/making-prototypes). The record structure is our implementation choice. Guidance reviewed: 16 November 2026.
