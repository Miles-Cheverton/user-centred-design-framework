## Prepare the brief

Confirm the audience, purpose and working folder. Keep fictional examples out of live briefings. For a current-position brief, check approved meeting sources, any handover the team supplied and relevant **Pending changes** in project config. If there is reason to expect a decision is missing from the records, ask for the relevant source or report the limit. Use [Find and explain](../find-and-explain/SKILL.md#answer-from-the-project-records) to trace what people learnt or decided. Give their agreed priorities and criteria with their basis. Mention a Withdrawn finding when it explains a change or leaves a gap in the evidence. Say which current claim needs review and distinguish any remaining evidence from the team’s reassessment. Use the [status and save rules](../../ai-guidance.md#check-status-and-what-was-saved) to label open or unsaved items. If using an unsaved update, cite the underlying source and say the register update is pending. Identify simulated behaviour, the scope of any fulfilment or verification result and open choices. [AI guidance](../../ai-guidance.md)

| Briefing | Include |
|---|---|
| Refinement | Proposed work, need, outcome, agreed criteria and open choices |
| Playback | Team findings, evidence limits and next steps the team proposed or agreed |
| Phase review | Key decisions, findings that support or challenge each one, remaining uncertainty and decisions the team has left open |
| Handover | Current decisions, exact design versions, key sources and recorded owners and next steps |

Use the [guidance for experienced teams](../../ai-guidance.md#advise-an-experienced-team) to choose what matters to the audience. For a release decision, put the user outcome beside what the team tested, what actually shipped, the effect on staff or other channels, and any dependency that could change the decision. Give an owner or review date when recorded. For a recommendation, distinguish the assistant's advice from the team's agreed position.

Return the brief in the requested format with source citations the audience can follow.
