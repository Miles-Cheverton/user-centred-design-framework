# How to use the project foundations

Start with the task in front of you. Use the [record catalogue](../README.md#find-a-record) if you already know what you need to find.

## Frame the work

1. Set the [working folder](../project-config.md). Bring in existing records with [Import an existing project](../AI-skills/import-existing-project/SKILL.md) when useful.
2. Write the [problem statement](files/problem-statement.md). Say who experiences the difficulty and why it matters.
3. Capture known users, outcomes and responsibilities. Record what is [in scope](../in-scope.xlsx) and [out of scope](../out-of-scope.xlsx), and why. [Register the sources](files/source-register.md) the team relies on.
4. Record important unknowns as [assumptions](files/assumptions.md) or [research questions](files/research-questions.md).

Agree where the team keeps the current record for each kind of information. Use [project config](../project-config.md#other-project-locations) to note any that live elsewhere. For example, keep delivery status on the team’s board and link to it from the relevant framework record.

## Capture learning

1. Before reading an AI summary, give the researcher, observers and other colleagues space to say what stood out. [Compare interpretations](evidence-and-interpretation.md#human-analysis-before-documentation) and note what the team concluded, where people disagree and what is still open.
2. While the group is still together, use [Capture or update records](../AI-skills/capture-or-update-records/SKILL.md) with those notes and any transcript or other sources approved for AI use. Ask it to propose the updates, find existing records that match and point to relevant passages the group may have missed. For example, it might flag a timestamp where a participant took a different route.
3. Discuss anything that could change the interpretation. Retain disagreements in the debrief and include any that limit a finding on its row. Save the agreed [findings](files/findings-and-observations.md) with exact sources, including contrary or inconclusive learning. Update related [user needs](files/user-needs.md), assumptions or research questions when the team agrees.

When sharing research more widely, use a summary that protects participants’ identities.

## Prepare research

Link the [questions](files/research-questions.md) and a short [protocol](research-protocol.md) from the [round](files/research-plan.md). Prepare the method and consent, and link the materials you use. Record the [sessions that take place](files/research-sessions.md), then analyse the evidence with the team.

## Explore a design

Retain the exact [journey](files/user-journeys.md) or [prototype](files/prototypes.md) versions used in research, review, decisions or handover. Use a [use case](files/use-cases.md) to describe a specific interaction. For an early policy or offline option, keep a version of the team’s design note or board when it will be useful later. [How to retain an option and trace the choice](files/decisions.md#retain-an-option-before-a-decision). Capture what the team learns in [findings](files/findings-and-observations.md).

## Assess whether needs are met

Use the existing [user need row](files/user-needs.md#assess-whether-the-service-meets-the-need) for the team’s fulfilment assessment, tested scope and evidence. Record its delivery choice independently. Before quoting workbook totals, check the warnings in row 1 and report the assessed scope and open gaps.

## Record a decision

Use the [decision guide](files/decisions.md) to record the choice, rationale, evidence and who made it. Update the records it affects. Keep earlier choices discoverable when a decision changes.

## Capture what changed in a meeting

1. Ask the delivery manager, business analyst or other person taking notes to capture significant choices, reasons and open questions. Use the same approach for a stakeholder call, design review or stand-up. Keep routine actions in the delivery backlog.
2. Give [Capture or update records](../AI-skills/capture-or-update-records/SKILL.md) those notes and, if available, the transcript approved for AI use. Ask it to propose changes to decisions, dependencies or other relevant records. It can find an exact passage, flag a mismatch and fill in a date or name when the source establishes it.
3. Review the proposed changes in the call or with the decision maker. Record an agreed choice with its scope, reason and any open conditions. Keep proposals open and flag unclear agreement for confirmation. Put an open start date in the decision’s **Why**. Link a dependency if an outside team or system needs to act first. Link to the exact source and timestamp or section. Use one [source register entry](files/source-register.md) for a source cited by several records.

## Hand over or prepare a review

Show the team’s current position, the evidence it rests on and what is still open. Cite exact design versions and relevant record IDs. Use [Prepare a briefing](../AI-skills/prepare-a-briefing/SKILL.md) to draft from those records.

## Find and check an answer

Find the record and read it with its status and source. [Check the claim](evidence-and-interpretation.md#check-a-claim) against the actual evidence and report any gaps. For AI assistance, use [Find and explain](../AI-skills/find-and-explain/SKILL.md).

## Capture a little, retain what matters

Capture the first useful record when the work happens. Fill in the details required by its file guide before relying on it. Use the team’s existing reviews to check significant findings and choices. When you run [Check evidence and records](../AI-skills/check-evidence-and-records/SKILL.md) for project integrity, it flags duplicate IDs and shows where they occur.

## Adapt the starter

Use the registers that help with your work. You can leave unused workbooks empty and use the team’s existing tools for the records they already own. Note those choices and any team rules in [project config](../project-config.md). A new colleague can start there.

For example, a small team might start with user groups, user needs, findings and decisions. Keep the session register when an observation cites an SES ID and the source register when it cites a DOC ID. Add other registers when you need them. Follow the [record links](record-conventions.md#canonical-relationships) for the work in scope.

You can add local notes and extra columns at the right of a table. Before renaming or removing files, changing headings, table names, dropdown values or ID patterns, check the affected links, skills, lookups and totals. Record any local mapping in project config. If you remove a workbook, also update the project README and check which records still refer to it. If you install a skill outside the project, give it access to the project guidance and records it needs.

## Upgrade an existing project

1. Read **Starter version** in your project README and find the file list below. Extract the new ZIP to a separate folder outside the project. Keep the working project in place. Keep a restorable copy of the files you will change. Find the original starter in the [GitHub releases](https://github.com/Miles-Cheverton/user-centred-design-framework/releases) if you need to compare local edits. Keep the ZIP you installed if you work offline.
2. Keep your project config, local notes, populated workbooks and sources. Update only the paths listed. Compare your copy with the old and new starter before replacing customised files. Keep the current **Starter version** line when merging the README.
3. Stage the skills, AI guidance, handbook, record conventions, linked file guides and examples together. Check each listed file against the target starter. If any dependency still needs review, keep the previous matched set in use, including any separately installed skills.
4. [Refresh the skills where you use them](../AI-skills/README.md#refresh-skills-after-an-upgrade). Check the revisions in the installed skills and both shared guidance files. Open the project's [Findings and observations guide](files/findings-and-observations.md#withdraw-a-finding) and compare its revision and withdrawal steps with the target starter. Check that **Withdrawn** is available in the working findings workbook. If anything is missing or you cannot verify it, keep the old **Starter version**.
5. Compare one existing need, its finding and source, and any linked decision or assessment with your pre-upgrade copy. Run the quick test. Set **Starter version** to the version in the downloaded starter once the checks pass.

If you stop partway through, compare the listed files with the target starter and finish what remains. Use the backup or version history to recover only the affected files. Check for newer team edits before restoring them.

### Files to update from v0.5.3

- Merge the starter `README.md`, keeping your project details. Keep your `project-config.md` and add the optional **Pending changes** location if the team already has an approved place for it.
- Update `ai-guidance.md`, `AI-skills/README.md` and the six `SKILL.md` files. Keep any skills your team added.
- Update `how-to/README.md`, `how-to/record-conventions.md` and the four guides `findings-and-observations.md`, `user-needs.md`, `decisions.md` and `source-register.md` under `how-to/files/`.
- Update `how-to/quick-test/example-findings-and-observations.xlsx` and `how-to/quick-test/example-session-notes-v1.md`. They are fictional.
- Keep your populated `findings-and-observations.xlsx`. The [Outside table warning](#add-the-findings-workbook-warning) is optional for existing projects.

For an older starter, use the [v0.4 migration notes](https://github.com/Miles-Cheverton/user-centred-design-framework/blob/v0.4/repository-info/CHANGELOG.md#upgrading-from-v037-or-earlier) first.

### Update the user needs totals and warnings

On a copy, confirm that the table is named `userNeeds` and uses the starter column names. Cells B1 to I1 should contain only starter totals or warnings, or be empty. If they contain local formulas or labels, keep them and use the integrity skill until you can adapt the formulas.

Copy the formula text from the new starter’s formula bar for each cell B1 to I1 and paste it into the matching cell in the copy so the table references resolve locally. Recalculate and check the totals, records and dropdowns. Once the test passes, repeat the edits on the latest working workbook. Keep any edits your colleagues have made in the meantime.

### Add Withdrawn to an existing findings workbook

1. On a copy, open **Records** and select the **Status** cells. The starter rule covers D4:D1003. Use the actual range in your workbook, including any longer table or separate ranges with the same rule.
2. Open **Data Validation** and add **Withdrawn** to the existing list. For the standard rule with comma-separated choices, use `Draft,Reviewed,Superseded,Withdrawn`. Keep existing local choices and use the separator that your rule already uses. If your Excel version offers **Apply these changes to all other cells with the same settings**, use it only for cells that share the intended Status rule. [Microsoft’s dropdown editing guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/add-or-remove-items-from-a-drop-down-list)
3. Check the new option and the existing records. Repeat the rule edit on the latest shared workbook, keeping any changes colleagues made while you tested. [How to record a withdrawal](files/findings-and-observations.md#withdraw-a-finding)

### Add the findings workbook warning

If C1 is free in your findings workbook and its table is named `findings` with the starter column names, copy just the C1 formula text from the starter. Test and recalculate on a copy. Repeat the edit in place on the latest working workbook once it passes. Keep any team content already in C1. The integrity skill can check for rows outside the table without this formula.
