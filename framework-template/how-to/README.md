# How to use the project foundations

Start with the task in front of you. Use the [document tracker](../document-tracker.xlsx) if you already know what you need to find.

## Frame the work

1. Set the [working folder](../project-config.md). Bring in existing records with [Import an existing project](../AI-skills/import-existing-project/SKILL.md) when useful.
2. Write the [problem statement](files/problem-statement.md). Say who experiences the difficulty and why it matters.
3. Capture known users, outcomes and responsibilities. Record what is [in scope](../document-tracker.xlsx) and [out of scope](../document-tracker.xlsx), and why. [Register the sources](files/source-register.md) the team relies on.
4. Record important unknowns as [assumptions](files/assumptions.md) or [research questions](files/research-questions.md).

Agree where the team keeps the current record for each kind of information. Use [project config](../project-config.md#other-project-locations) to note any that live elsewhere. For example, keep delivery status on the team’s board and link to it from the relevant framework record.

## Capture learning

1. Before reading an AI summary, give the researcher, observers and other colleagues space to say what stood out. [Compare interpretations](evidence-and-interpretation.md#human-analysis-before-documentation) and note what the team concluded, where people disagree and what is still open.
2. While the group is still together, use [Capture or update records](../AI-skills/capture-or-update-records/SKILL.md) with those notes and any transcript or other sources approved for AI use. Ask it to propose the updates, find existing records that match and point to relevant passages the group may have missed. For example, it might flag a timestamp where a participant took a different route.
3. Discuss anything that could change the interpretation. Retain disagreements in the debrief and include any that limit a finding on its row. Save the agreed [findings](files/findings-and-observations.md) with exact sources, including contrary or inconclusive learning. Update related [user needs](files/user-needs.md), assumptions or research questions when the team agrees.

When sharing research more widely, use a summary that protects participants’ identities.

## Prepare research

Link the [questions](files/research-questions.md) and a short [protocol](research-protocol.md) from the [round](files/research-plan.md). Prepare the method and consent, and link the materials you use. Record the [sessions that take place](files/research-sessions.md), then analyse the evidence with the team.

## Explore a design

Retain the exact [journey](files/user-journeys.md) or [prototype](files/prototypes.md) versions used in research, review, decisions or handover. Use a [use case](files/use-cases.md) to describe a specific interaction. For an early policy or offline option, keep a version of the team’s design note or board when it will be useful later. [How to retain an option and trace the choice](files/decisions.md#retain-an-option-before-a-decision). Capture what the team learns in [findings](files/findings-and-observations.md).

## Assess whether needs are met

Use the existing [user need row](files/user-needs.md#assess-whether-the-service-meets-the-need) for the team’s fulfilment assessment, tested scope and evidence. Record its delivery choice independently. Before quoting workbook totals, check the warnings in row 1 and report the assessed scope and open gaps.

## Record a decision

Use the [decision guide](files/decisions.md) to record the choice, rationale, evidence and who made it. Update the records it affects. Keep earlier choices discoverable when a decision changes.

## Capture what changed in a meeting

Record selectively. A stakeholder meeting, design review or technical discussion may be worth recording if the team will need to revisit the reasons for a choice, the options it considered or the conditions it agreed. Use the delivery board for routine stand-up actions and blockers. If a significant decision comes up in any meeting, capture it while the people involved are there.

Keep any recording and transcript in the team’s approved workspace. Follow your organisation’s rules on recording, access and retention. Use the team’s agreed notes and decisions as the starting point for handover. Link to the transcript where it helps someone check or recover the detail.

1. Have the delivery manager, business analyst or other note-taker capture what the team agreed, why and what is still open.
2. Give [Capture or update records](../AI-skills/capture-or-update-records/SKILL.md) those notes and, if available, the transcript approved for AI use. Ask it to propose changes to decisions, dependencies or other relevant records. It can find an exact passage, flag a mismatch and fill in a date or name when the source establishes it.
3. Review the proposed changes in the call or with the decision maker. Record an agreed choice with its scope, reason and any open conditions. Keep proposals open and flag unclear agreement for confirmation. Put an open start date in the decision’s **Why**. Link a dependency if an outside team or system needs to act first. Link to the source you used. If you used the transcript, link to it and give the timestamp or section. Use one [source register entry](files/source-register.md) when several records cite the same source.

**Example**. In a stakeholder meeting, the delivery manager notes an agreed decision and a proposed start date. The AI assistant checks the approved transcript, finds the reason for the decision and gives the timestamp. The delivery manager checks with the decision maker, who confirms the date is still open. The assistant records the agreed choice and rationale and leaves the date open.

## Hand over or prepare a review

Show the team’s current position, the evidence it rests on and what is still open. Cite exact design versions and relevant record IDs. Use [Prepare a briefing](../AI-skills/prepare-a-briefing/SKILL.md) to draft from those records.

## Find and check an answer

Find the record and read it with its status and source. [Check the claim](evidence-and-interpretation.md#check-a-claim) against the actual evidence and report any gaps. For AI assistance, use [Find and explain](../AI-skills/find-and-explain/SKILL.md).

## Capture a little, retain what matters

Capture the first useful record when the work happens. Fill in the details required by its file guide before relying on it. Use the team’s existing reviews to check significant findings and choices. When you run [Check evidence and records](../AI-skills/check-evidence-and-records/SKILL.md) for project integrity, it flags duplicate IDs and shows where they occur.

## Adapt the starter

Use the registers that help with your work. You can leave unused workbooks empty and use the team’s existing tools for the records they already own. Note those choices and any team rules in [project config](../project-config.md). A new colleague can start there.

For example, a small team might start with user groups, user needs, findings and decisions. Keep the session register when an observation cites an SES ID and the source register when it cites a DOC ID. Add other registers when you need them. Follow the [record links](record-conventions.md#canonical-relationships) for the work in scope.

Use the [document tracker](../document-tracker.xlsx) as the authoritative list of current record locations. The **Record** column contains the starter filename or the name of an additional file. Keep the starter record name and update **File or URL** if it moves, using a path relative to the working folder or an approved exact URL. Update the clickable hyperlink too, or remove it if it would point elsewhere. Use **What it holds** for the content and any relevant scope. Mark a deliberately unused record's **File or URL** as Unused. When records are split between tools, identify each owner's scope in the tracker or project config. Resolve conflicting owners before using either.

Add rows to the same table for useful additional files such as handover notes or workshop outputs. You do not need to list every attachment or repeat source citations. A tracker entry does not make a file authoritative evidence or grant access. Check other project-specific links or source citations affected by a move, preserving exact source versions and record IDs.

You can add columns to a workbook. Give each one a clear, unique heading and add it inside the Excel table so it stays with its record when you sort or filter. If an older local column is outside the table, verify which ID each value belongs to before relying on it or incorporating it. Keep the starter headings and IDs. Before removing a file or changing headings, table names, dropdown values or ID patterns, check what relies on them. Note local mappings in [project config](../project-config.md).

## Add the tracker to an existing project

Copy `document-tracker.xlsx` into the working project. Keep your existing records and carry over the current paths or URLs and descriptions from your README’s file list. If you already have a tracker, merge the starter record names and retain your current locations, local ownership and extra rows. If the previous tracker was a Markdown file, transfer its entries to the workbook and verify them before removing the old tracker. Check missing or conflicting destinations and update the README to link to the workbook. Use the matching AI skills, AI guidance and record conventions.

## Upgrade an existing project

1. Read **Starter version** in your project README and find the file list below. Extract the new ZIP to a separate folder outside the project. Keep the working project in place. Keep a restorable copy of the files you will change. Find the original starter in the [GitHub releases](https://github.com/Miles-Cheverton/user-centred-design-framework/releases) if you need to compare local edits. Keep the ZIP you installed if you work offline.
2. Keep your project config, local notes, populated workbooks and sources. Update only the paths listed. Compare your copy with the old and new starter before replacing customised files. Keep the current **Starter version** line when merging the README.
3. Stage the skills, AI guidance, handbook, record conventions, linked file guides and examples together. Check each listed file against the target starter. Check that the [document tracker](../document-tracker.xlsx) maps the current records to the right locations, including local or external records. If a dependency still needs review, keep the previous matched set in use, including separately installed skills.
4. [Refresh the skills where you use them](../AI-skills/README.md#refresh-skills-after-an-upgrade). Check the actual installed skills and both shared guidance revisions against the target starter. Compare the [Findings and observations guide](files/findings-and-observations.md#withdraw-a-finding) with the target. Check the applicable workbook controls for retained registers, including Withdrawn and updated warnings. For another owning tool, verify equivalent status handling and record any local mapping. If a register is deliberately unused, skip its live checks and keep the tracker or local convention clear about that choice. Keep the old **Starter version** if an applicable check is incomplete.
5. Compare a representative existing record, its evidence and linked decisions or assessment with the pre-upgrade copy when they exist. If the project is empty or does not hold that record chain, check whichever records it does retain and run the fictional quick test. Run the quick test in a fresh chat with the actual skills in use. Set **Starter version** to the version in the downloaded starter once the applicable checks pass.

If you stop partway through, compare the listed files with the target starter and finish what remains. Use the backup or version history to recover only the affected files. Check for newer team edits before restoring them.

### Add Withdrawn to existing registers

On a copy of each register your team uses, find the **Status** column and its data validation rule. The starter ranges and values are below. Use the actual range and table in your working workbook.

| Register | Starter range | Standard choices |
|---|---|---|
| User needs (`user-needs.xlsx`) | E4:E1003 | Proposed, Evidenced, Superseded, Withdrawn |
| Research questions (`research-questions.xlsx`) | D4:D1003 | Open, Investigating, Answered, Superseded, Withdrawn |
| Business rules (`business-rules.xlsx`) | D4:D1003 | Proposed, Approved, Superseded, Withdrawn |
| Non-functional requirements (`non-functional-requirements.xlsx`) | D4:D1003 | Proposed, Agreed, Superseded, Withdrawn |

Add Withdrawn to the existing list in **Data Validation**, preserving local options and using its actual separator. Extend the rule to cover the table where necessary. Verify that current values stay valid and the new choice is available. Do the same to the latest working workbook, keeping team edits. A withdrawal requires a team-confirmed reason; the upgrade itself does not change any record's status.

### Update the user needs totals and warnings

On a copy, confirm that the table is named `userNeeds` and keeps the standard core headings and their positions. If the team customises ID patterns, table names, core field positions or formulas, adapt the warnings to match its conventions and verify them with test rows. Cells B1 to I1 should contain only starter totals or warnings, or be empty. If they contain local formulas or labels, keep them and use the integrity skill until you can adapt the formulas.

Copy the formula text from the new starter’s formula bar for each cell B1 to I1 and paste it into the matching cell in the copy so the table references resolve locally. Recalculate and check the totals, records and dropdowns, including a withdrawn need and a row missing its ID or evidence. Once the test passes, repeat the edits on the latest working workbook. Keep any edits your colleagues have made in the meantime.

### Add Withdrawn to an existing findings workbook

1. On a copy, open **Records** and select the **Status** cells. The starter rule covers D4:D1003. Use the actual range in your workbook, including any longer table or separate ranges with the same rule.
2. Open **Data Validation** and add **Withdrawn** to the existing list. For the standard rule with comma-separated choices, use `Draft,Reviewed,Superseded,Withdrawn`. Keep existing local choices and use the separator that your rule already uses. If your Excel version offers **Apply these changes to all other cells with the same settings**, use it only for cells that share the intended Status rule. [Microsoft’s dropdown editing guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/add-or-remove-items-from-a-drop-down-list)
3. Check the new option and the existing records. Repeat the rule edit on the latest shared workbook, keeping any changes colleagues made while you tested. [How to record a withdrawal](files/findings-and-observations.md#withdraw-a-finding)

### Add the findings workbook warning

If C1 is blank or holds the previous starter warning, and your table is named `findings` with the standard core headings and positions, copy just the C1 formula text from the new starter. Test and recalculate on a copy, including a row below the table populated only in an evidence or status field. Repeat the edit in place on the latest working workbook once it passes. Keep any team-specific content already in C1. The integrity skill can check for rows outside the table without this formula.
